# Sandbox Retrospective — Four Leaks, One Species (2026-10-03)

**TL;DR: four measurement bugs, the same species, and the sandbox's own pre-registered gates killed every one before any cost a rupee. Every strategy number this sandbox ever celebrated was produced by a bug, and the fourth one killed the last survivor. The machinery is the asset; the strategies were the noise. This file is the permanent record and the rulebook for the next search.**

The second leak (contract identity, found 2026-10-02) voided the sandbox's second-best result and every experiment built on it. **A third was found on 2026-10-03 (e026): the repo's one surviving candidate was marked entirely on a model, and re-marking it to real prices cut it 6.7× — from +₹4,14,721 to +₹61,843.** That leak was not a bug in a search; it was an absence of an audit, and it was invisible to every gate in the repo because every gate looked at PnL. **A fourth was found the same day (e028), by auditing the third's own input: e026's loader compared a `date` to a raw string, so four years of real marks never loaded and its "64 verified sessions" was half the sample.** Corrected, the candidate is **−₹29,082 per year-and-a-bit at a realistic fill** — dead. This revision is the complete record: e001–e028, the surviving inventory, and the rules the next search inherits.

> **⚠ 2026-10-03 restatement, twice over.**
> 1. e013's published **+₹4,14,721 is struck** (e026): audited **+₹61,843** on 64 real-marked sessions.
> 2. ~~That +₹61,843 is also struck~~ (e028): its sample was **format-selected by an expiry-encoding defect**. Corrected on **129** sessions: **+₹45,568** at the engine's own 0.75-pt slippage, **−₹29,082 at a realistic 2.0 pts/leg**, breakeven **1.51 pts/leg**, **93.0%** of PnL from 2025. **e013 is DEAD.** Full records: [e026](experiments/e026_realmark_audit/), [e028](experiments/e028_expiry_encoding_audit/).

## 1. What the day-t wall leak actually was

One convention, inherited silently through four generations of code: **walls = max-OI strikes of day t's own EOD option chain**. It entered at e001 (mirroring the original engine's labels), was kept by e005 deliberately ("leg-for-leg comparison to the frozen labels"), and became load-bearing when the breach book made the wall-crossing test its entire signal. The problem: the entry happens at 09:15, but the walls are computed from OI as it stood at **15:30** — 6 hours of information the live trader cannot have. The "spot gapped over the wall" event exists only against walls that moved into the way *during* the day being traded. The book's entire edge was the wall update itself.

Three properties made it dangerous, in ascending order of lesson value:

1. **It was invisible to every statistical gate.** Calibration was excellent (Brier ≈ 0.20) *because* the models faithfully predicted the leaky labels. Win rates of 98.8% raise suspicion only after a leak is suspected; before that, they look like skill. **Calibration measures fidelity to the labels, not truth of the labels.**
2. **It compounded through inheritance.** e001 → e002 labels → e005 walls → breach book → e006 compounding → the handoff. Each consumer added a "caveat" line, none re-derived the foundation. By the time the number reached ₹940,697, six documents vouched for it and none had checked it.
3. **It survived because the sandbox measured the right things on the wrong object.** Marks were validated to the paisa, friction was real, exits were honest — every *price* was genuine. Only the *selection signal* was fictional. Honest components ≠ honest assembly.

## 2. The second leak: contract identity

[experiments/e011_vrp_delta_hedge/volatility.py](experiments/e011_vrp_delta_hedge/volatility.py) built its implied-volatility signal by taking **the expiry nearest to day t-1**, inverting that ATM straddle, and then shifting the result by one row. The shift was correct; the *instrument* was not. The contract tradable on day t is the expiry nearest to **t**. When t-1 was an expiry day, those are different contracts, and the pinned straddle got inverted with a tenor ~1/5 of its real life. `e018` re-derives the same measurement on 293 E011 signal sessions:

| | E011 (convicted) | contract-identity-correct |
|---|---:|---:|
| mean signal IV | 0.476 | **0.163** |
| max signal IV | 1.929 | **0.473** |
| sessions IV > 1.00 | 35 | **0** |
| sessions IV ≥ 0.60 | 68 | **0** |
| mean tenor credited | 0.95 days | **4.71 days** |
| roll-day enrichment in the signal | **2.55×** | **1.00×** (none) |

Three things make this the more instructive of the two leaks:

1. **The published PnL was not inflated by optimism — it was measured on a contract that did not exist.** +₹7,43,572.25, Sharpe 2.85, PF 3.99 across 293 trades. Every rupee of it is a bisection artifact.
2. **The gate selected on the bug.** Roll sessions are 21% of trading days but **54%** of E011's signals — the entry rule fired 2.55× more often on exactly the days its own inversion had broken. The strategy was not trading a regime; it was trading its instrumentation error. Correct the instrument and the selection disappears entirely.
3. **It propagated the same way the wall leak did.** e015, e016 and e017 each froze e011's artifacts and reasoned carefully *on top of them* — a book combination, a wings test, a sizing study, three papers deep. The contract-identity defect was inherited silently by each. All three are void. ~~e013 is clean: it replays only genuine `dte ≤ 1` sessions, so the contract identity holds by construction.~~ **This sentence was believed for two days and was measured false on 2026-10-03: only 61 of e013's 193 sessions are `dte ≤ 1`.** See §2A — and note the pattern: a re-implementation inherits the predecessor's *claims about itself* unless someone measures them.

**Why it survived the first leak's autopsy.** The sandbox had built a whole apparatus against `t-1` discipline — registry rows, `t+1`-fill tests, shift-pinned feature tests — and all of it was satisfied. `t-1` is a claim about **when** an input was observed. It says nothing about **which instrument** the observation belongs to. A shifted frame can hand you yesterday's tenor. The first bug was a wrong *time*; the second was a wrong *object*, wearing a correct timestamp.

**Same species, stated once:** both bugs are a silent convention inherited through generations of consumers, each of which added a caveat line instead of re-deriving the foundation. A convention cannot stop this, because the convention is what broke. **A test is the only thing that stops it** — and the test has to be written against the *quantity*, not the intent:

- `test_front_expiry_roll_is_not_yesterdays_contract` — the front expiry is resolved out of the trade date's own partition, so identity is a fact, not an inference.
- `test_tenor_matches_the_contract_not_a_stale_offset` — observed tenor must strictly *lead* traded tenor; e011's failure was the opposite sign.
- `test_no_degenerate_iv_in_the_dataset` — the signal's own distribution is checked. **A mean IV of 0.476 with a max of 1.93 is not a strategy result, it is a bug report.** Nobody had ever plotted the shape of the signal; every gate in the repo looked at PnL.
- `_identity_ok` re-derives the intended expiry from the trade date's partition on **every trade** and gate 4 fails the run on a single mismatch. 0 violations across 141 trades.

## 2A. The third leak: the marks were never prices (found 2026-10-03)

**This one is different from the other two, and it is the one that almost cost the whole program.** The first two bugs produced a *wrong signal*. This one had a *right signal*, correctly executed, honestly costed — and marked with a model instead of a market.

e013's +₹4,14,721 was composed entirely of `bs_call` / `bs_put` outputs on a single stale t-1 implied vol. **No real option price entered the number at any point.** Every gate in the repo inspected that number's PnL, its profit factor, its Sharpe, its drawdown and its win rate. None of them asked whether the inputs were prices.

The measurement, from [e026](experiments/e026_realmark_audit/):

| | Net | PF | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Published (Black-Scholes marks) | +₹4,14,721 | 9.41 | 193 |
| **Audited (real bhavcopy closes)** | **+₹61,843** | **16.67** | **64** |

Three properties make this the most instructive of the three:

1. **It was an absence, not an error.** Nothing in e013 was wrong. The selection was clean by construction, the timing respected t+1 fills, the friction model was real, the lot eras were correct. The signal is probably still there. What was missing was the one check nobody wrote: *is this number made of prices?*
2. **Every existing gate passed it.** PF 9.41, WR 83.9%, DD 5.97%, Sharpe 5.52 — a clean bill of health from every metric the repo knew how to compute. §5.11 ("suspicion scales with roundness") was the only instrument that even *pointed* at it, and it had been noted as a standing caveat rather than acted on. **A gate suite that only measures PnL cannot detect a PnL composed of the wrong objects.**
3. **The repo already had the tool and had not used it.** e018 built the arithmetic-impossibility audit specifically to catch marks that are not prices. It found a ₹1,04,000 phantom on e018. It was never pointed at e013 — the *surviving* book. The audit existed; the application did not.

**And the compound finding:** e013 was believed "clean by construction" because it "replays only genuine `dte ≤ 1` sessions." Measured: **61 of 193.** 132 sessions trade contracts with days of life left and carry **76% of the PnL** — and the book marks their exit at `t = 0.0001`, i.e. as if the options expired fifteen seconds after the exit. That convention is defensible for the 61 expiry-day sessions and indefensible for the rest. **The label was inherited from the intent and never checked against the artifact — the same species as the other two, one level up.**

**The rule this adds:** *marks are an input, not an output.* Any book whose PnL is a function of a pricing model rather than observed prices must be marked to observed prices before it is believed, and that check must be written **before** the result is known. e026's PREREG did exactly this, and its control arm — which reproduces e013 to the paisa — is what makes the 6.7× haircut attributable to the mark and to nothing else.

## 2B. The fourth leak: the real marks never loaded (found 2026-10-03, e028)

**The audit of the fix was the bug's last hiding place.** e026 replaced modelled marks with real ones. It did not then check that the real ones it loaded were *the real ones*.

One line, in e026's loader:

```python
n = n[n["expiry"] == str(front_expiry)]      # a datetime.date, compared to a string
```

`str(date)` is `2023-12-20`. The bhavcopy store holds **two encodings**: `04-Feb-2021` (2021–2024) and ISO `2025-01-02` (2025+). The comparison was true for **zero** pre-2025 sessions. Measured across the store: **356 of 576** wall sessions returned an empty front chain. All four legs were `None`, and §5.5's fail-closed rule — **behaving perfectly** on the wrong answer it was handed — dropped each session as `NO TRADE`.

Four properties, in ascending order of lesson value:

1. **A fail-closed rule is not a correctness argument.** The guard is the thing this repo is proudest of, and it did its job: it refused to invent a mark. What it could not do was notice that the question it was refusing was the wrong one. **Fail-closed protects you from fabricating data; it does nothing about fabricating the question.** 356 sessions vanished without a log line, because *no data* and *wrong lookup key* return the same empty frame.
2. **It was invisible in aggregate and obvious as a curve.** Marks existed at a ~70% share in 2025 and a **0%** share in 2023. Any single coverage number would have looked merely mediocre. The per-year cut is what betrays it — and §2.1's audit already proved that lesson on a missing partition, which is the uncomfortable part.
3. **It had been inherited once already.** e027 built its own copy of the same loader. `e018` never had the bug, because it builds a parsed `expiry_date` column rather than filtering a string. The defect survived precisely where nobody was looking, which is to say *in the copy that had just been written.*
4. **It inverted the finding of the leak above it.** e026 concluded the book "survives real marks" on 64 sessions. Corrected: the book **loses ₹29,082** at a realistic fill on 129. The recovered sessions are *worse on average* than the ones the bug kept — EV/trade falls from +₹966 to +₹353 even though total PnL is only cut 26%. **The bug was not hiding a bad book behind a good one. It was hiding a large bad book behind a small good-looking one.** The most dangerous form of a sampling defect is the one that improves the headline.

**And it corrupted the record downstream.** `RETROSPECTIVE.md` §5.11 carried three load-bearing claims that this file itself wrote: that no pre-July-2024 session has a valid mark, that valid-mark share rises 38.7% → 56.4% → 70.0%, and that 91% of PnL comes from 2025. **All three were artifacts of the bug, and §5.11's conclusion — "annualise over the calendar, never over the surviving sample" — was inferred from a measurement failure dressed as a fact about the market.** The rule survives; its evidence does not. That is written up as §5.11 below.

**The rule this adds:** *publish the input's coverage before the result.* A lookup returns `(frame, reason)`, never a bare empty frame. Coverage is reported as a number, **per era**, before any PnL that depends on it. And the defect is fixed at the shared root — `core/feeds/bhavcopy.py`, which now attaches a parsed date column, raises on a frame with no `expiry`, and writes ISO at the normalize boundary — with the defective version **kept and labelled** as e028's control arm, so the correction has to reproduce the predecessor to the paisa before its own numbers count.

**What survives from e026 is the method and none of the sample.** The control arm reproduced +₹61,842.51 exactly, which is why the 129-session delta is attributable to the encoding and to nothing else. That is the whole value of the *control-reproduces-predecessor* rule (§5.9): it is what let a two-line fix be treated as a finding rather than as a patch.

## 3. How they were caught — and why that part worked

**None of the four was caught by reviewing numbers. All four were caught by asking a question the frozen pipeline had never asked** — and each was then fixed as a **pre-registered hypothesis test rather than a patch**, so the fix got a verdict instead of a pass.

The wall leak: the paper-trade harness asked *what could the trader actually know at 09:15?* Walls from t-1 EOD bhavcopy — the only observable source — collapsed the book (57 trades, −₹27.5k). Pre-registered gate 0 finished the proof with the freshest possible observable source (opening-OI walls via the per-bar-OI API): 4 trades, −₹309, plus the decisive diagnostics — opening walls differ from t-1 walls on 74% of days (no "stale copy" excuse) and the opening structure is valid on 244 of 249 "breach" days (the signal has nothing to fire on).

The contract-identity bug: a **brainstorm** asked a question no gate ever had — *which contract is this PnL actually about?* It was not a review pass, not a refactor, not a data audit. It was the question "shifted by one, is that the same object?" — and no test in the repo asked it, because every test asked the same question the code did.

Then the fix was deliberately **pre-registered as a hypothesis test, not a patch**: e018's PREREG froze six gates on Option 1's own terms (weekly 3–7 DTE condor held to expiry) before any code ran. The fix got a verdict instead of a pass. It failed — and that failure is the reason we know the fix is real rather than merely plausible: a fix that had been tuned to reproduce +₹7.43L would have been a worse fix.

The structural lesson, now three times over: **the gates that worked were the ones written as commitments before the answer was known.** The gates that failed were diagnostics run on the artifact itself (calibration, PF, WR, marks, Sharpe).

**The third leak was caught by the same species of question.** A brainstorm asked *is this PnL made of prices, or of a model's opinion of prices?* — the third instance of "name the object". No test asked it, because every test asked the same question the code did. And it was **pre-registered as a hypothesis test, not a patch**: e026's PREREG froze six gates, including the rule that if gates 3 *or* 4 fail the published figure is struck. It passed — and the passing verdict still restates the number downward, which is the same property e018 demonstrated. A fix tuned to reproduce +₹4.14L would have been a worse fix than one that does not.

**The fourth leak was caught by auditing the third's own input**, which is the only thing that found it. The question was not new — it is the §5.10 question one level down: *is this input actually there?* Nobody asked it of e026 until e026's own verdict became the last surviving candidate and therefore the last thing worth attacking. **A fix is only as good as the audit of the fix.** e026's gates 0–6 all passed; gate 7 (added post-run, non-voiding) then found 52 of 772 legs closing more than 4 ticks below intrinsic — the only correction in the whole experiment that made the book *worse* than both the published and the corrected-corrected number, and it was found last, by not being willing to write a report on a clean result.

## 5. What a clean strategy search does differently

1. **Live-replicability is the design constraint, not a validation step.** Every pipeline starts with "what information exists at decision time?" answered *before* the strategy is written. If the answer needs future data, the strategy dies in design, not in gate 0.
2. **The leak registry is constitutional.** No PnL number enters the record without a declared information set (`t-1` / `day-t` / `none`) and a live-replicability flag; day-t ⇒ not live, enforced by test. New experiments inherit this the way they inherit the sandbox policy. The E011 conviction is a registry row now, so it cannot be quietly inherited a third time.
3. **Pre-register kill criteria before the first run, and make them cheap to fail.** e008's scope did this right: signal, data ceiling, and three kill bars written *before* the fetch. It died in one afternoon for the cost of a verdict script. e019 died the same way, for the cost of a data download. That is what a healthy kill looks like.
4. **Suspicion scales with roundness.** 98.8% WR, PF 62.6, a max IV of 1.93 — the smoother the story, the harder the look. Real books have ugly tails. Treat any WR > 85% on a credit book, or any implied volatility above 1.0, as presumptively broken until the information-set audit clears it.
5. **The t+1-fill hard rule is permanent.** Signal at bar t, fill at bar t+1's observable quote, with the quote's freshness recorded.
6. **One data class per question.** e008 died not of look-ahead but of *observability* — the only data class that can see the signal cannot carry the fills. Match the data's freshness and completeness to the signal's clock before falling in love with a hypothesis.
7. **Name the instrument, then shift the frame.** A `shift(1)` is a claim about *when*, not *what*. Any signal row must be able to answer "which contract, which strike, which expiry, which tenor" from the trade date's own partition, and a test must re-derive that answer per row. Corollary: **publish the signal's distribution before its PnL.** A histogram costs nothing and has now caught a bug that moved ₹7.43 lakh.
8. **Benchmark first; gate on excess, not on level.** Every long-only equity book is reported against a same-universe equal-weight benchmark over the same days with the same costs, and the bar is on excess Sharpe. "24.8% CAGR" and "3.1% CAGR" in the same table were both beta; only the benchmark column says so.
9. **A control beats a diagnostic.** Two cheap checks that produce decisions where fifty PnL metrics produce nothing: (a) *the successor must reproduce the predecessor's number to the decimal before its new numbers are believed*; (b) *any structure with a closed-form maximum loss must be checked against it per trade* — violations are pricing bugs, not tail events.
10. **Marks are an input, not an output. (added 2026-10-03, from e026 — the costliest lesson here.)** Any book whose PnL is a function of a pricing model rather than observed prices must be re-marked to observed prices before it is believed, and that re-audit must be **pre-registered and controlled** (freeze the signal, move only the mark, require the control arm to reproduce the predecessor to the paisa). This is not a refinement of rules 7 and 9 — it is a new class of leak that those rules cannot see, because it hides behind a *correct* signal and a *fully honest* PnL. Every gate the repo owned passed +₹4,14,721. The one instrument that even pointed at it, rule 4, had been filed as a caveat instead of acted on. **A gate suite that only measures PnL cannot detect a PnL made of the wrong objects.**
11. **Annualise over the calendar, never over the surviving sample. (added 2026-10-03 from e027; **evidence struck and rule re-stated** by e028 the same day.)** A filtered sample almost never spans the period it is annualised over, and the filter is rarely random. **The rule stands — but the evidence this file originally gave for it was fabricated by a bug.** ~~The 64 real-marked sessions span 2.14 years but are quoted as 5.4%/yr by dividing into the full 5.71-year calendar; the valid-mark share rises monotonically 38.7% → 56.4% → 70.0% by year; no pre-July-2024 session has a valid mark at all.~~ **Every one of those numbers was an artifact of e026's expiry-encoding defect.** There was no era skew; there was an empty chain. Corrected coverage is **100.00%** of markable sessions (129/129) across all six years, spanning the full 5.71 years. What survives is the *shape* of the rule and two genuinely alarming facts that survive the correction: the recovered 2021–2023 sessions are **losing** (−₹7,487 / −₹1,589 / −₹6,173), and **93.0% of net PnL comes from 2025** — worse concentration than the 91% e027 reported on its half-sample. **Report the by-year cut before the average, state which denominator the annualisation uses, and never cite a filter's extent as evidence of the filter's bias until you have verified the filter ran.**
12. **Publish the input's coverage before the result; an empty frame must say why. (added 2026-10-03, from e028 — the rule that voided e013.)** A chain lookup returns `(frame, reason)`, never a bare empty frame; "no such session" and "wrong lookup key" are different answers. Coverage is reported as a number, **per era**, before any PnL that depends on it, and is never inferred from the size of the result. Three consequences, all learned the expensive way: **a fail-closed rule is not a correctness argument** (356 sessions were dropped silently for four years, correctly, on a wrong answer); **coverage is audited per year, not in aggregate** (the bug was invisible in total and obvious as a curve); and **defects are fixed at the shared root, never in an experiment**, with the defective version retained and labelled as the successor's control — because e026 and e027 each carried their own copy of the same bug and a local fix would have left the third copy live. **This is rule 10 one level down: marks are an input, so the *loading* of marks is an input too, and it deserves the same suspicion.** *Now enforced, not just written: `tests/test_feeds.py::TestBhavcopyStoreCoverage` walks every one of the 1,415 partitions on disk and asserts a non-empty NIFTY front chain resolves, reporting coverage per year. Reintroducing the defect makes it fail with the exact curve that betrayed it.*
13. **Hold out an era before you slice one. (added 2026-10-03, from e029.)** Any claim that a *subset* of a book works is scored on a period the subset was not chosen from. e029's contract froze selection on 2021–2023 and the verdict on 2024–2026 with no overlap, carried **at most one** filter across, and required the result to beat the **95th percentile of 500 same-size random subsets** — not merely zero. **A positive number an arbitrary subset of equal size also reaches is not an edge; it is arithmetic.** Two corollaries this repo paid for directly: a **filter that is really a date filter** is the commonest form of this failure (e029's `credit ≥ median` selects 100% of the late era and ~half the early one — a calendar wearing a threshold), and **an era split chosen by looking at the eras has no holdout at all** and cannot be rescued by better statistics later.
